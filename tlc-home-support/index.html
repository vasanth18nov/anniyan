Programme Philosophy

The Home Support Programme should be viewed as an independent-living learning programme, with the homestay environment serving as the context in which students practise real-world skills.

Rather than staff completing tasks for students, the programme uses a “support, teach, fade” approach, with adult assistance decreasing as each student demonstrates competence — while appropriate safety and supervision are maintained throughout.

→
We do
→
You do with support
→
You do independently

Staff involvement decreases left to right as student independence grows

Weekly Programme Structure

Monday — Transition, Planning & Dinner

Regular school day — 3:00 PM transition to Home Support. Students practise packing away school belongings, checking their timetable, transitioning calmly, changing into comfortable clothes, organising personal belongings and reviewing the evening's activities.

Grocery shopping & meal planning: create a shopping list, check existing ingredients, identify required items, compare prices, select quantities, use a basket/trolley, pay, check receipts, carry groceries and store food appropriately.

Dinner preparation: washing ingredients, food preparation according to ability, measuring, cooking, setting the table, serving, washing dishes, wiping surfaces and storing leftovers. Roles should rotate.

Optional leisure: music, dancing, board games, movies, exercise, art, reading, social activities or quiet individual time. Students practise making their own choices.

Tuesday — Morning Routine & Functional Independence

7:00 – 8:30 AM · Independent morning routine using a visual checklist

  • Wake up
  • Make bed
  • Personal hygiene
  • Get dressed
  • Organise belongings
  • Check timetable
  • Prepare and eat breakfast
  • Wash dishes / clean eating area
  • Prepare school bag
  • Check personal items

Breakfast progression:

Simple foods→ Toast / eggs→ Sandwiches / wraps→ Cooked breakfast→ Independently planned & prepared

Students also practise portioning, food hygiene, appliance safety, cleaning, food storage and checking expiry dates.

9:00 AM: Regular classes.

Tuesday Afternoon & Evening — Community Independence (from October)

3:00 PM · Return to Home Support · Rotating weekly life-skills theme

WeekFocus
1Grocery shopping
2Cooking
3Money & budgeting
4Community access
5Laundry & clothing care

Tuesday community dinner: students practise choosing where to eat, setting a spending limit, reading a menu, making their own choice, ordering, communicating with staff, paying, checking the receipt/change and reflecting afterwards.

“What did I do independently today?” · “What was difficult?” · “What would I like to try myself next time?”

Wednesday — Independent Living Skills

Repeat breakfast and morning routines while intentionally fading prompts. Before school, students complete a “Reset the Home” routine: make bed, put clothes away, place dirty laundry in the basket, clear the breakfast area, wash dishes, wipe the table, check belongings and leave shared spaces tidy. Then attend regular classes at 9:00 AM and return home after school.

Six Core Independence Domains

Personal Independence

Hygiene, dressing, grooming, sleep routines, managing personal belongings

Domestic Skills

Cooking, food preparation, cleaning, laundry, household organisation

Financial Independence

Recognising money, budgeting, comparing prices, paying, checking change, purchasing decisions

Community Independence

Navigating familiar environments, schedules, ordering food, safety, social interaction, choice-making

Executive Function & Self-Management

Schedules, time management, planning, problem solving, decision-making, asking for help

Social & Emotional Independence

Communicating needs, managing frustration, peer negotiation, turn-taking, coping with change, boundaries, minor conflict resolution

Least-to-Most Support Model

All staff should use a consistent prompting hierarchy to reduce prompt dependency:

1

Wait

Allow time for the student to initiate

2

Visual prompt

Use a checklist, photo, symbol or schedule

3

Gesture

Point or gesture toward the relevant task

4

Verbal prompt

Give a brief, clear instruction

5

Demonstration

Model the action when needed

6

Physical assistance

Only when necessary — for safety or access

Prompt-fading example

Monday“It's time to get dressed.”
Tuesday“Check your morning checklist.”
WednesdayStudent independently checks the schedule

Student Ownership & Shared Responsibilities

Students should have genuine ownership of the programme. Responsibilities can be divided and rotated so that each student learns multiple skills rather than becoming dependent on a fixed role.

This week

Student A

Manages the shopping list

Prepares food during dinner

This week

Student B

Organises groceries

Cooks during dinner

Roles rotate the following week

The long-term aim is for students to experience the space as “our home” with shared responsibilities, rather than as a place where adults provide services.

Staff Roles & Programme Positioning

The programme should clearly communicate that overnight support is not simply supervision. Staff provide structured teaching, guidance, safety support and opportunities for students to practise real-life skills.

“Teacher Jack will lead the programme, supported by an additional staff member during overnight hours. Staff will provide appropriate supervision, guidance and safety support while encouraging students to complete tasks as independently as possible. Individual levels of support will be adjusted according to each student's needs, with the aim of gradually reducing adult prompting as students develop greater confidence and competence.”
Recommended programme wording

Recommended Pilot Approach

Beginning with Irfan and Matin provides an opportunity to establish programme systems before expanding to 3–4 students.

  • Individual baseline assessments for each student's independence skills
  • Visual schedules and task checklists
  • Consistent prompting and prompt-fading procedures
  • Rotating household responsibilities
  • Weekly skill targets
  • Independence Passport progress records
  • Student reflection after key activities
  • Regular communication with families about progress and next goals

Overall Programme Outcome

Success should ultimately be measured by increased student independence: what students can initiate, complete, manage, communicate and problem-solve with less adult assistance over time.

Three questions for every student

1

What could the student do at baseline?

2

What level of support does the student currently require?

3

What is the next skill we are intentionally working toward?
